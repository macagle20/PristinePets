Start here

When I started Pristine Pets, I knew I wanted to work with dogs and serve my community. What I didn’t know was how all the pieces of a mobile grooming business would fit together. Should I buy into a franchise or build my own business? How much could I afford to spend on a van, and how would I get a business loan? How would I set up a business bank account and keep the money separate from my personal finances?

Then there was the work of getting open. What insurance did I need? How would I take bookings and payments, keep track of taxes, and find my first customers? And once I had a groomer, how was I going to keep their schedule full enough that they’d want to stick around? It’s easy to picture a day grooming dogs and miss how much work goes into making that day happen.

I’ll walk you through the work I did and the decisions I made while building Pristine Pets. I’ll tell you what worked, what I’d change, and what I wish I’d known sooner. You’ll get practical next steps for your own business. If what I learned helps you make even one better decision than I did, that’s exactly why I wrote this guide.

One thing I wish more people said up front about starting a business: expect to get some things wrong. You’ll make decisions you’d make differently the second time around. That’s part of it. What matters is noticing when something isn’t working, figuring out what you can change, and taking the next step.

You don’t have to read this straight through. If you’re still deciding whether to go mobile, start at Step 1. If you already have a van and want to fill your schedule, jump to Step 7. And if you’re grooming on your own with no plans to hire, skip Step 5.
